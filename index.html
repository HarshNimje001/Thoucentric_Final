PROBLEM STATEMENT

What problem is EIC actually trying to solve?

The company faces a demand–network alignment paradox. EV registrations are growing at approximately 32% annually, but demand is uneven across locations and customer segments. While 68% of EV owners prefer home charging, commercial fleets rely heavily on public charging. therefore, EV ownership growth cannot be treated as a uniform proxy for public charging demand. Private operators are raising expectations around accessibility, reliability and charging speed. With one investment to be selected within the ₹5,000 Cr capital envelope toward 2030, the decision is how to deploy capital to solve the mismatch rather than simply increase network size.

02

ROOT-CAUSE DRILL-DOWN

Why does the mismatch persist?

  1. W1
    Why underutilization?

    Some stations may have more capacity than their immediate public-charging demand.

  2. W2
    Why congestion elsewhere?

    Public-charging demand is concentrated among specific customer segments and locations.

  3. W3
    Why aren't fleets filling every highway station?

    Fleet demand depends on actual operating routes and charging behaviour.

  4. W4
    Why can mismatch persist?

    Physical charging assets are location-specific while demand changes across regions, segments and peak periods.

  5. W5
    Why does this matter?

    Without continuous demand-led planning and capacity management, initial allocation gaps persist.

FIRST PRINCIPLES

A charging station is not valuable just because it exists.

It creates value when it is accessible, available, operational and used.

Need to charge → Suitable station → Available → Operational → Low friction → Successful session

The fundamental objective is not to maximize charger count — it is to maximize reliable charging sessions from available physical capacity and capital.

ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS

The 5 Whys points to a potential gap in the granularity and responsiveness of the company's network planning and capacity allocation process. Fixed capacity may not be sufficiently aligned with evolving area level, customer segment and peak period demand, producing metropolitan congestion alongside underutilized semi-urban and highway assets. Commercial fleet demand may also follow routes that do not match existing infrastructure. Because charging infrastructure is location-specific and difficult to relocate or resize after deployment, a mismatch can persist.

STRATEGIC RECOMMENDATION

Option C — Intelligent Charging Network Modernization

Option C ranks first at 4.20/5, ahead of B at 3.05 and A at 3.00, primarily because it directly addresses the root cause.

Recommendation is option C. At Rs. 2,600 Cr over three years, it combines demand forecasting, smart load balancing, predictive maintenance, reservations, remote monitoring, analytics, dynamic pricing and renewable-energy integration. It targets a 30% increase in station utilization while improving availability, reliability and network responsiveness.

The three options involve clear trade-offs: Option A increases fast-charging capacity but requires Rs.4,800 Cr and may not solve the demand-location mismatch; Option B improves geographic accessibility but requires Rs. 3,900 Cr with uncertain demand in new locations; Option C offers the strongest root-cause fit at the lowest investment of Rs 2,600 Cr, but depends on technology, data quality and organizational adoption.

The recommendation does not reject future physical expansion. Instead, it uses improved demand intelligence and network utilization to identify locations where additional capacity is genuinely required. This reduces the risk of premature investment while keeping the network scalable through 2030.

Option C does not solve the complete problem on its own, especially the physical capacity and geographic coverage gaps. However, among the available options, it is the best fit for the current scenario because it directly addresses the underlying demand network alignment issue while preserving flexibility for targeted expansion later.

Risk with option C: Customers may face inconvenience during the transition due to system changes, inaccurate availability information or initial technical issues.

Solution: Roll out in phases, conduct pilot testing, provide real time support and ensure customers can easily access reliable charger availability and booking information.

ANALYTICAL GUARDRAILS

How we avoid overclaiming

📌

Case fact

Directly stated by the case. Example: 2,800 stations, 46% utilization, ₹5,000 Cr budget.

🔗

Inference

A logical interpretation derived from multiple case facts. Example: the coexistence of congestion and underutilization suggests a demand-capacity mismatch.

🔮

Assumption

A bridge used because the case does not provide enough granular evidence. Always label it.

🎯

Recommendation

The team's strategic judgement after diagnosis, comparison and stress-testing.

🚫
Do not invent: urban/rural penetration percentages, competitor market shares, revenue, ROI, station economics, charger failure rates or unsupported demand forecasts. If the case does not provide it, say "not provided in the case."

ABOUT THE WORK

Why this strategy is conditional

"None of the three options is perfect. C is the best fit only under the stated assumptions."

The decision is not "C instead of expansion forever." It is a sequencing choice: modernize the network, learn where public demand is actually emerging, improve today's performance, and use the resulting evidence to decide where physical expansion is genuinely required in the next capital cycle.
